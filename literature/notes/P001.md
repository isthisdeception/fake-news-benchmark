## Title
Fake News Detection: Comparative Evaluation of BERT-like Models and Large Language Models with Generative AI-Annotated Data

## Authors
Shaina Raza (AI Engineering, Vector Institute, Toronto, ON, Canada); Drai Paulen-Patterson (Department of Computer Science, Toronto Metropolitan University); Chen Ding (Department of Computer Science, Toronto Metropolitan University)

## Year
2024 (arXiv:2412.14276v2 [cs.CL], 20 Dec 2024)

## Venue
Not Reported (preprint posted on arXiv: arXiv:2412.14276v2 [cs.CL])

## DOI
Not Reported (arXiv id: arXiv:2412.14276v2)

## Research Objective
"This study presents a comparative evaluation of BERT-like encoder-only models and autoregressive decoder-only large language models (LLMs) for fake news detection." The work introduces a news-article dataset labeled with GPT-4 assistance and verified by human experts, fine-tunes both model families on it, and aims "to determine which model family offers superior classification capabilities and under what conditions one may outperform the other."

## Research Questions
- RQ1: Which families of models, BERT-like models or LLMs, perform better for the task of fake news detection?
- RQ2: Are GPT-powered labels more accurate compared to source-level labels?
- RQ3: How do different families of models perform under adversarial perturbations, and which models demonstrate better robustness?

## Dataset(s)
- Own dataset: news articles curated via Google RSS (political news, May 2023–Oct 2023), labeled for fake news detection with GPT-4 Turbo assistance and verified by human experts (GPT-powered labels).
- NELA-GT-2022 dataset [78]: a multi-labeled benchmark news corpus using distant/source-level supervision (veracity inferred from Media Bias/Fact Check ratings).

## Dataset Size
- Own dataset: ~30,000 news articles curated via Google RSS; a sample of 10,000 articles (those with more than 150 words) selected and labeled using AI. The paper also states: "The curated dataset consists of 40,000 news articles labeled for fake news detection and the labeled dataset is 10,000." The 10,000 annotated samples were split 80-10-10 (train/validation/test).
- NELA-GT-2022: full corpus contains 1,778,361 articles from 361 outlets (Jan 1, 2022–Dec 31, 2022); the authors selected a subset of 10,000 articles (minimum length 50 words), with an 80-20 train-test split.

## Language(s)
Not Reported explicitly (news articles; NELA-GT-2022 described as a news corpus). No explicit language named for the datasets.

## Task
Binary fake news classification (fake/real news detection). Also uses GPT-4 Turbo for binary (fake/real) data annotation.

## Model(s)
- BERT-like encoder-only models: DistilBERT (base, uncased), BERT (large/base, uncased), RoBERTa (large / base-uncased as reported in tables).
- Autoregressive decoder-only LLMs: Llama2-7B-Chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2.
- Annotator model: GPT-4 Turbo (used for labeling, not as classifier).

## Architecture Type
Encoder-only transformers (BERT-like) with a softmax/sigmoid classification layer over transformer output; and autoregressive decoder-only transformer LLMs (instruction fine-tuned, with majority voting over 5 prompts during inference). LLMs fine-tuned with PEFT and 4-bit quantization via QLoRA.

## Preprocessing Pipeline
- Data curated via Google RSS using `feedparser` and `newspaper.Article` API (extracting article texts, publication dates, outlets, URLs).
- Selected articles with more than 150 words (own dataset); minimum length 50 words for the NELA-GT-2022 subset.
- Annotation via GPT-4 Turbo in a two-shot demonstration mode (binary labeling), followed by human review (8 reviewers over 4 weeks; disagreements resolved by consensus; Inter-Rater Reliability ≈ 72%).
- For LLM training, data converted into an instruction format with two-shot demonstrations.
- SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) applied to address class imbalance for both BERT-like models and LLMs.

## Training Strategy
- BERT-like models fine-tuned (PyTorch + Hugging Face Transformers) with early stopping.
- LLMs (Llama2-7B-Chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2) instruction fine-tuned using PEFT and 4-bit quantization via QLoRA (bitsandbytes + HF Trainer); QLoRA config: 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4), double quantization, paged optimizers.
- Inference for LLMs: each article prompted 5 times; final label via majority voting; average confidence computed; if average confidence exceeds threshold (0.8), majority label accepted.
- 5-fold cross-validation used; best model selected on lowest validation loss.
- Compute: one NVIDIA A100 and four NVIDIA A40s (32GB RAM each).

## Hyperparameters
BERT-like Models:
- Models: DistilBERT (base, uncased), BERT (large, uncased), RoBERTa (large)
- Learning Rate: 2e-5 to 5e-5 (DistilBERT, BERT), 1e-5 to 4e-5 (RoBERTa)
- Batch Size: 16-32
- Epochs: 20 (early stopping)
- Optimizer: AdamW
- Weight Decay: 0.01
- Warm-up Steps: 6% of total steps (RoBERTa only)
- Classification Threshold: 0.5 (default for all) [note: text elsewhere states a threshold of 0.8 applied to probabilities for final classification]

LLM Variants:
- Models: Llama2-7B-chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2
- Learning Rate: 1e-5 to 3e-5
- Batch Size: 8 (Training), 16 (Evaluation)
- Epochs: 5 (early stopping at 1; more led to overfitting)
- Optimizer: AdamW
- Weight Decay: 0.01

QLoRA Parameters: lora r = 64, lora alpha = 16, lora dropout = 0.2; Task Type: CAUSAL LM; Bias: None; BitsandBytes: use 4bit = True, bnb 4bit dtype = float16, bnb 4bit quant = nf4, use nested quant = True.

Best ablation config: batch size 16, learning rate 3e-5.

## Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score. Reported as mean and standard deviation on the test set; 5-fold cross-validation. Also inference time (ms), throughput (examples/sec), and training time (hrs). A threshold of 0.8 is applied to probabilities/confidence scores for final classification.

## Baseline Models
The two model families are compared against each other (BERT-like vs LLMs), and GPT-powered labels (own dataset) vs source-level distant labels (NELA-GT-2022). LLMs evaluated under zero-shot, few-shot (5 examples), and instruction fine-tuning settings. Models: BERTBase-Uncased, DistilBERTBase-Uncased, RoBERTaBase-Uncased, Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.2.

Robustness to text perturbations (Table 8, "Correct per Model" out of 15 examples): BERT 10, RoBERTa 10, DistilBERT 10, Llama2-7B 14, Mistral-7B 14. LLMs maintained higher accuracy under 'Drop', 'Insertion', 'Negation', 'Antonym', 'Typo', 'Shuffle' perturbations.

Key findings: BERT-like models (esp. RoBERTa) outperform LLMs in classification; GPT-powered (human-supervised) labels outperform source-level distant labels; LLMs are more robust under text perturbations. (Note: the Discussion text at one point states "LLMs outperform BERT-like models (RQ1)", which contradicts the rest of the paper and tables where BERT-like models are reported as better classifiers.)

## Strengths
- Provides a controlled comparison of two model families (BERT-like vs LLMs) on the same task with the same annotation pipeline and SMOTE handling.
- Introduces a GPT-4-annotated + human-verified dataset (10,000 labeled articles) and compares AI+human labels vs distant/source-level labels.
- Reports robustness/adversarial evaluation using the TextAttack library across many perturbation types.
- Uses 5-fold cross-validation and reports multiple metrics with mean/standard deviation; releases annotation method and code.

## Weaknesses
- Inconsistent reporting: the RQ1 Discussion statement ("LLMs outperform BERT-like models") contradicts the tables and abstract (which state BERT-like models generally outperform LLMs in classification).
- Some dataset numbers are inconsistent (30,000 curated vs "40,000 news articles" in the schema section; 10,000 labeled).
- Language(s) of the dataset not explicitly stated; dataset available only "upon request"/from first author.
- Threshold reporting is inconsistent (Table 3 states classification threshold 0.5 "default for all", while metrics text uses 0.8).

## Limitations
Stated by the authors: (1) 4-bit QLoRA quantization on LLMs may cause some performance degradation vs full-precision BERT-like models, which were run without such memory management; (2) did not evaluate other LLMs such as Claude or GPT-3.5/4 as classifiers (GPT-4 Turbo used as annotator; weights not public); (3) analysis limited to LLM responses—other prompting techniques (e.g., chain-of-thought) not explored; non-instruction versions of Llama/Mistral (capable of sequence classification) not examined; (4) annotation method has limitations for production-level annotation; (5) confidence scores from LLMs differ from discrete probabilities of BERT-like models, complicating integration; (6) multilingual and multimodal contexts not explored.

## Future Work
Stated by the authors: integrating active learning (model iteratively queries a human annotator or uses an LLM as annotator) for production-level annotation; exploring additional prompting techniques (e.g., chain-of-thought); examining non-instruction versions of Llama/Mistral for sequence classification; exploring multilingual and multimodal contexts; optimizing prompting techniques for LLMs and integrating multimodal data to further improve fake news detection.

## Interesting Ideas
- LLM-as-annotator with human verification (GPT-4 Turbo two-shot labeling + 8-reviewer expert/student consensus; IRR ≈ 72%) as a scalable labeling method.
- Majority voting over 5 LLM prompts plus average-confidence thresholding (0.8) to stabilize stochastic LLM classification outputs.
- Direct comparison of AI+human labels vs distant/source-level supervision on the same task.
- Systematic adversarial robustness testing (TextAttack) showing LLMs degrade less under perturbations.

## Possible Inspiration For Our Paper
- For the Fake News Benchmark: adopt both an "annotation quality" axis (AI+human vs distant supervision) and a "robustness" axis (adversarial perturbations via TextAttack) in addition to raw classification metrics.
- Include the majority-voting + confidence-threshold protocol as a standardized inference method for decoder-only LLMs in the benchmark.
- Use their hyperparameter tables as a reference configuration for fine-tuning BERT/RoBERTa/DistilBERT and QLoRA fine-tuning of Llama2-7B / Mistral-7B baselines.

## Potential Citation Usage
- Cite as evidence that encoder-only BERT-like models (esp. RoBERTa) can outperform 7B decoder-only LLMs on fake news classification, while LLMs offer superior adversarial robustness.
- Cite for the AI-annotation-with-human-oversight labeling methodology and for QLoRA-based efficient LLM fine-tuning in fake news detection.
- Cite as a data point in a benchmark's related-work comparison of label sources (GPT-powered vs source-level).

## Reviewer Notes
Strong methodological template for a benchmark (multiple model families, cross-validation, robustness testing, ablations). Caution: watch the internal inconsistency in the RQ1 conclusion and the dataset-size discrepancies (30k vs 40k vs 10k) and threshold values (0.5 vs 0.8) when quoting exact numbers. Venue/DOI are absent (arXiv preprint only). Dataset is not openly downloadable (available upon request from first author), which affects reproducibility for benchmarking purposes.
