Enhancing fake news detection with transformer-based deep learning: A multidisciplinary approach

## Research Objective
To propose a robust fake news detection framework centered on a BERT-based transformer architecture enhanced with a progressive (episode-based) training methodology that incrementally learns and refines its understanding of linguistic nuances distinguishing factual reporting from fabricated content, relying exclusively on textual content (not metadata).

## Research Questions
Stated research question: "Can a progressive, episode-based training methodology significantly improve the performance and robustness of a BERT-based model for fake news detection when compared to a standard, single-run fine-tuning approach?"

## Dataset(s)
WELFake dataset (a combination of four news datasets: Kaggle, McIntire, Reuters, and BuzzFeed Political). Comparative analysis also references PolitiFact and GossipCop datasets from prior works.

## Dataset Size
WELFake: 72,134 news articles — 35,028 real and 37,106 fake. Format: CSV with 4 columns (Serial number, Title, Text, Label); Label values 0 (Fake), 1 (Real).

## Language(s)
English

## Task
Binary fake news classification (real vs. fake).

## Model(s)
- Proposed: BERT-based framework with progressive/episode-based transfer training ("BERT-Transform-based Learning" / Transfer Learning based model)
- Baseline: standard BERT fine-tuning (single run)
- Uses HuggingFace Transformers classes BertTokenizer and BertForSequenceClassification; described foundation model as "Bert-base-uncased" (paper text states "124-layer, 1024-hidden, 16-head, 340M parameter, Bert-base-uncased pre-trained model")

## Architecture Type
Transformer-based deep learning (BERT encoder) fine-tuned for binary sequence classification; enhanced with a progressive/iterative (episode-based, checkpoint-resumed) training strategy.

## Preprocessing Pipeline
- Exploratory data analysis (EDA): data distribution, class balance, data quality analysis, descriptive statistics and visualizations
- Data cleaning: handling missing values
- Text cleaning: removal of special characters, lowercasing
- Stemming / lemmatization (reducing words to root form)
- Stop word removal (e.g., "the", "and", "in")
- Tokenization (BertTokenizer)
- Batching and shuffling

## Training Strategy
- Progressive / episode-based training: trained iteratively in stages with checkpointing — first 10 episodes (weights saved), reloaded and trained 10 more episodes, then reloaded and trained 5 more episodes, for a total of 25 episodes.
- Baseline: standard single-run BERT fine-tuning for 25 epochs.
- Optimizer: AdamW (paper also mentions Adam/SGD as commonly used and a learning rate scheduler such as CosineAnnealingLR or ReduceLROnPlateau).
- Train-validation split with held-out test set; DataLoader preparation; learning rate scheduler used to prevent overfitting.
- Metrics monitored: accuracy, loss, F1, recall, precision; best-performing model selected then evaluated on test set.

## Hyperparameters
- Training episodes: 25 (progressive: 10 + 10 + 5); baseline: 25 epochs single run.
- Optimizer: AdamW.
- Output labels = 2 (true/false); model set not to output attentions/hidden states.
- Validation loss progression cited from a high value down to final 0.442 (referenced over "181 epochs" in one passage).
- Training time (progressive/transform-based, Table 5): 4:14:55 (HH:MM:SS); validation time 00:27:30. Baseline (without transform-based learning, Table 4): training time 9:53:47; validation time 57:50.
(Exact numeric learning rate, batch size, and max sequence length are Not Reported.)

## Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, validation/training loss, confusion matrices; correlation analyses among metrics (accuracy vs. precision, precision vs. F1, recall vs. accuracy); accuracy/precision/recall/F1 frequency-bin distributions; Circos diagram visualization.

## Baseline Models
- Internal baseline: standard BERT fine-tuning (single-run, non-progressive), 25 epochs.
- External comparison models (Tables 6, 9, 10, 11, 14): TCN-URG (Feng Qian et al. 2018; Aggarwal et al. 2020), MPFN (Jing Jing et al. 2023), FNR-S (Faeze Ghorbanpour et al. 2021), LIWC (Wani et al. 2021), CSI (Qian et al. 2018), HAN (Pennebaker et al. 2015), SAFE Multimodal (Ruchansky/Runchansky et al. / Zhou et al. 2020), BERT and BERT+LSTM (Nishant Rai et al. 2022).

## Main Results
Proposed model (progressive/transfer-learning), final test set: Accuracy 95.3%, Precision 0.952, Recall 0.954, F1-score 0.953.
Ablation (Table 3): Baseline standard BERT training — Accuracy 94.1%, Precision 0.940, Recall 0.942, F1 0.941; Proposed progressive training — Accuracy 95.3%, Precision 0.952, Recall 0.954, F1 0.953 (improvement of ~1.2% in accuracy and ~1.2% in F1).
BERT without transform-based learning (Table 4): Training Loss 0.001, Validation Loss 0.539, Validation Accuracy 0.96, Validation F1 0.948, Validation Precision 0.95, Validation Recall 0.94; training time 9:53:47.
BERT with transform-based learning (Table 5): Validation Loss 0.442, Validation Accuracy 0.953, Validation F1 0.953, Validation Precision 0.952, Validation Recall 0.954; training time 4:14:55.
Comparison table (Table 6): "BERT-based Framework (Proposed)" — 96% accuracy, 0.95 precision; "Transfer Learning based model (Our Proposed)" — 95.3% accuracy, 0.952 precision. Cited external models included e.g., BERT (Nishant Rai et al.) 86.25%, BERT+LSTM 88.75%, MPFN 83.30%, FNR-S 88.00%, SAFE 87.4%.
(Note: the paper reports both 96% and 95.3% accuracy in different places — 96% is the validation accuracy of one framework variant, 95.3% is the final held-out test accuracy of the progressive model.)

## Strengths
- Uses a large-scale, balanced dataset (WELFake, 72,134 articles) for robust evaluation.
- Text-only approach independent of metadata (user engagement, source credibility), improving applicability where metadata is unavailable.
- Clear ablation isolating the contribution of the progressive training strategy (+1.2% accuracy/F1 over standard fine-tuning).
- Progressive training notably reduced training time (4:14:55 vs. 9:53:47) while improving/ maintaining accuracy.
- Extensive comparison against many prior models and metrics.

## Weaknesses
- Reporting inconsistencies: accuracy stated as both 96% (validation) and 95.3% (test) in different sections/tables, which can confuse the headline result.
- Several exact hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, max sequence length) not reported.
- The described BERT foundation model specs ("124-layer, 1024-hidden, 16-head, 340M parameter, Bert-base-uncased") are internally inconsistent/erroneous (these mix up BERT-base vs. BERT-large numbers).
- Related-work section and some table captions contain garbled/typo-laden text and duplicated/mismatched captions (e.g., repeated "highly correlated" captions), reducing clarity.
- Single-dataset evaluation (WELFake only) limits cross-domain generalization claims.

## Limitations
Stated: the authors note the WELFake dataset's real and fake articles may not be distributed equally and news-record identity may not be fully accurate; comparisons across studies use different datasets/models so findings may not hold fully against each other. (No dedicated limitations section beyond these caveats.)

## Future Work
Stated by the authors: increase interpretability using SHAP or LIME; optimize performance via hyperparameter tuning; try other model architectures such as RoBERTa or DeBERTa; implement ensembling for better classification; extend to multilingual and multimodal data (images and social media metadata); improve model adaptation to changing misinformation patterns.

## Interesting Ideas
- Progressive / episode-based training with checkpoint reloading (10 + 10 + 5 episodes) as a lightweight strategy to boost robustness and cut training time versus single-run fine-tuning.
- Text-only, metadata-independent framing for broad applicability.
- Metric-correlation and frequency-bin analyses (and Circos visualization) to argue consistency of performance across metrics.

## Possible Inspiration For Our Paper
- WELFake (72,134 articles, balanced) is a strong candidate dataset to include in a fake-news benchmark; the note documents exact class counts.
- The progressive-training vs. single-run ablation is a reusable protocol for benchmarking training strategies, not just architectures.
- Training-time reporting (progressive halves training time) supports adding efficiency metrics to the benchmark.

## Potential Citation Usage
- Cite for a BERT + progressive/episode-based training approach on WELFake achieving ~95.3% test accuracy / 0.953 F1.
- Cite for WELFake dataset composition (Kaggle, McIntire, Reuters, BuzzFeed Political; 35,028 real / 37,106 fake).
- Cite as a recent (2025) transformer FND baseline for comparison, and as an example of a text-only, metadata-independent detector.
