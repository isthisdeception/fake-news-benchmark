## Title
Multi-Modal Anomaly Detection in Review Texts with Sensor-Derived Metadata Using Instruction-Tuned Transformers

## Authors
Khaled M. Alhawiti (Faculty of Computers and Information Technology, University of Tabuk, Tabuk 47713, Saudi Arabia)

## Year
2025

## Venue
Sensors, 2025, Volume 25, Article 7048 (MDPI, Basel). Academic Editor: Naveen Chilamkurti. Received 14 September 2025; Revised 1 November 2025; Accepted 11 November 2025; Published 18 November 2025.

## DOI

## Research Objective
To present a zero-shot framework for multi-modal anomaly detection (fake review detection) in user reviews, integrating textual and metadata-derived signals through instruction-tuned transformers. It fuses (i) language perplexity scoring with FLAN-T5, (ii) unsupervised reconstruction via a transformer-based autoencoder, and (iii) semantic drift analysis, cross-validated with device-level metadata (timestamps, product usage logs, operational signals), to enable label-free, domain-agnostic, interpretable fake review detection in e-commerce and IoT-enabled platforms.

## Research Questions
Not Reported (no explicitly enumerated research questions; stated as key contributions instead).

## Dataset(s)
- Primary: Amazon Reviews 2023 (McAuley-Lab, via Hugging Face Datasets).
- Supplementary metadata-rich sources: Amazon Review Data 2018 and Historic Amazon Reviews (1996–2014).
- External public fake-review benchmarks (zero-shot cross-benchmark evaluation): Amazon Fake Reviews (2022) [39] and Yelp Chi / Yelp Review Full [40].

## Dataset Size
- Amazon Reviews 2023: over 570 million reviews across 33 product categories, spanning 1996–2023 (text says reviews published 1999–2023 in one section, 1996–2023 in the dataset summary).
- Working subset: a representative subset of 1.2 million reviews sampled for balanced coverage (Table 2: Electronics 150,000; Books 120,000; Home & Kitchen 110,000; Beauty & Personal Care 90,000; Toys & Games 80,000; Others aggregated 650,000; Total 1,200,000; overall 15.0% anomalies).
- Also described: a representative subset of ten categories, each ~100,000 reviews (~1 million-review subset), with 15% synthetically perturbed anomalies per category.
- Yelp Review Full: 650k training reviews + 50k test reviews (5-class sentiment format).
- Comparative test set for Figure 9: balanced set of 1000 reviews from multiple Amazon domains.

## Language(s)
English only (non-English reviews removed via langdetect; FLAN-T5 optimized for English).

## Task
Zero-shot (and few-shot) multi-modal fake review / anomaly detection — binary classification of reviews as anomalous vs. normal, using text plus metadata (proxy for sensor-derived signals).

## Model(s)
- FLAN-T5 (instruction-tuned transformer; Base and XL variants) for zero-shot prompt inference, perplexity, and semantic embeddings.
- Custom transformer-based autoencoder (4-layer symmetric encoder–decoder with multi-head attention) for unsupervised reconstruction (referred to as VAE in one section).
- Hybrid anomaly scoring module fusing perplexity P(x), reconstruction error E(x), semantic drift Δ(x), and metadata score S(x).
- Interpretability: token-level saliency via input–gradient products / attention rollout, plus Gradient L2 Norm, Integrated Gradients (IG), and LIME.

## Architecture Type
Hybrid, modular, zero-shot multi-modal architecture: instruction-tuned transformer (FLAN-T5) + transformer autoencoder + latent semantic-drift alignment + metadata-informed scoring, fused via weighted linear combination and thresholding.

## Preprocessing Pipeline
Remove HTML tags, emojis, and special characters; lowercasing and punctuation normalization; tokenization with NLTK; strip non-English reviews via langdetect. Text normalized with Unicode NFC; tokenized with SentencePiece (T5-compatible) into token IDs, padded/truncated to fixed length 512; retain only English reviews with token count n ≥ 20. Prompt construction: insert review into an instruction template (e.g., "Determine whether the following review is anomalous, inconsistent, or machine-generated. Review: <review text>"). Synthetic anomalies injected at 15% rate via token shuffling, synonym substitution (WordNet), random repetition/omission of high-frequency tokens, and category mismatch; perturbation parameters calibrated against Amazon Fake Reviews (2022) and Yelp Chi statistics (n-gram entropy, sentiment polarity, length distribution).

## Training Strategy
FLAN-T5 operates in pure zero-shot mode (no gradient updates / no fine-tuning). The transformer autoencoder is trained unsupervised only on normal (verified, rating–sentiment consistent, typical-length) reviews for 10 epochs on 1.2 million reviews (~9 hours), assuming most historical reviews are genuine. Category-wise 80/20 train/validation split, ensuring test reviews are disjoint from AE training. Hybrid weights (α, β, γ, δ) tuned via grid search on synthetically perturbed held-out validation only (no labeled anomalies); weights fixed across evaluations, with only the decision threshold τ empirically determined (95th percentile of validation scores). Few-shot adaptability tested separately with 3–18% labeled samples for benchmarking only.

## Hyperparameters
- Hybrid score weights: α = 0.4 (perplexity), β = 0.3 (reconstruction), γ = 0.3 (semantic drift); optimized vs. equal-weight (0.33, 0.33, 0.33) baseline; constraint α+β+γ+δ = 1.
- Metadata fusion weights: (wt, wv, ws, wc) = (0.30, 0.15, 0.30, 0.25); saturation constants κt = 3, κs = 1, κc = 0.5; missing-metadata penalty η = 0.05.
- Max sequence length: 512 tokens; minimum token count: n ≥ 20.
- Autoencoder: 4-layer encoder–decoder; trained 10 epochs (~9 h) on 1.2M reviews.
- Threshold τ: static or 95th-percentile (quantile) of validation scores.
- Inference: FLAN-T5 ~0.41 s per review (≈2450 samples/h), inference memory < 12 GB VRAM.
- Software: Python 3.10; PyTorch (text says 2.2, Table 3 says 2.1); Hugging Face Transformers v4.40; NumPy, Scikit-learn; PyTorch Lightning v2.5.6; CUDA 12.1 (Docker); Weights & Biases logging.
- Hardware: text reports NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48 GB VRAM), AMD Threadripper 3970X (32 cores, 3.7 GHz), 256 GB RAM; Table 3 instead lists NVIDIA RTX 4050 (32 GB VRAM), Ubuntu 22.04 LTS (internal inconsistency noted).
- Training epoch tracking (Table 6): AE loss 0.690→0.320 and FLAN-T5 perplexity 18.2→9.6 over epochs 50→250.

## Evaluation Metrics
AUC, F1-Score / F1-Macro, Precision (Precision@k / Precision@100), Recall, confusion-matrix outcomes (TP/FP/TN/FN), domain-shift robustness, cross-domain stability (std of AUC), token-level agreement/interpretability score, and saliency visualization.

## Baseline Models
LSTM-Autoencoder (LSTM-AE), One-Class SVM, and Prompt-BERT. (GPT-4-based detectors and adversarially tuned RoBERTa-large mentioned as strong baselines but deferred to future work as they fall outside the zero-shot objective.)

## Main Results
- Headline (abstract/contributions): strong zero-shot performance with AUC up to 0.945, and few-shot adaptability AUC > 0.95. (Note: the experimental body reports somewhat lower maxima — see below.)
- Per-category zero-shot (Figure 2): AUC 0.860 (Automotive) to 0.935 (Electronics); F1-Macro 0.670 to 0.740.
- Ablation (Figure 5): FLAN-T5 only AUC 0.875; AE only 0.861; FLAN-T5 + AE 0.902; Full Hybrid 0.918.
- Weight configuration (Figure 6): equal-weight (0.33/0.33/0.33) AUC 0.912; optimized (0.4/0.3/0.3) 0.928; High-P Low-Δ (0.5/0.4/0.1) 0.922; Bias-Δ (0.25/0.25/0.5) 0.914.
- Cross-domain (Figure 4): AUC 0.82–0.94 for Train-on-X/Test-on-Y.
- Domain-shift robustness (Table 4): in-domain vs. avg cross-domain AUC with 3–6% drop, e.g., Electronics 0.931→0.874 (6.13%), Books 0.904→0.864 (4.42%), Home 0.894→0.866 (3.13%).
- Few-shot (Table 7): AUC 0.872→0.933, F1-Macro 0.792→0.861, Precision@100 0.841→0.892 as labels increase 3%→18%.
- Baseline comparison (Table 8): Proposed cross-domain AUC 0.87 vs. LSTM-AE 0.72, One-Class SVM 0.65, Prompt-BERT 0.79; domain-shift robustness 0.07 (lowest); token-level agreement 0.76; few-shot F1 (@10 samples) 0.82; std of AUC across domains 0.031 (most stable).
- Cross-benchmark on labeled fake-review datasets (Table 9): Proposed (Hybrid) AUC 0.918, F1 0.803, Precision@100 0.864, Recall 0.756; Prompt-BERT 0.881/0.763/0.832/0.708; LSTM-AE 0.768/0.654/0.695/0.603; One-Class SVM 0.709/0.597/0.640/0.561.
- Saliency (Table 5): example top token attributions — "sound" (LIME 0.8659), "gripping" (Grad L2 0.8995), "size" (Grad L2 0.9391).

## Strengths
- Label-free zero-shot design that avoids costly anomaly annotation while still generalizing across 10+ product domains.
- Novel fusion of three complementary textual signals (perplexity, reconstruction error, semantic drift) plus metadata, rather than using them in isolation.
- Built-in interpretability via token-level saliency (gradient, IG, LIME) and metadata-level attribution.
- Extensive empirical validation: per-category, cross-domain, ablation, weight-sensitivity, few-shot, baseline comparison, and external cross-benchmark tests.
- Large-scale, reproducibility-friendly setup (public Amazon Reviews 2023, documented environment, fixed weights, threshold-only calibration).

## Weaknesses
- "Multi-modal / sensor-derived metadata" uses only structured Amazon fields (timestamp, verified, rating, category) as proxies — no genuine sensor/IoT time-series data is used.
- Anomalies are largely synthetic (token shuffling, synonym substitution, repetition, category mismatch) rather than real spam or LLM-generated fakes, limiting realism.
- Internal inconsistencies: hardware (RTX A6000 48 GB in text vs. RTX 4050 32 GB in Table 3), PyTorch version (2.2 vs. 2.1), and headline AUC (up to 0.945 / few-shot >0.95 in abstract vs. ≤0.935 in-domain and ≤0.933 few-shot in the results tables).
- Strong recent baselines (GPT-4 detectors, adversarial RoBERTa-large) are omitted.
- Equation cross-references are occasionally mislabeled (e.g., metadata term references "Equation (5)").

## Limitations
Stated by the author (Conclusions/Future Work): current experiments use structured metadata fields as surrogates for sensor signals rather than actual time-series sensor traces, limiting the true multi-modal/IoT scope; a detailed ablation of the metadata term's marginal benefit is not yet provided; the autoencoder assumes most historical reviews are genuine, so residual fraudulent samples may bias the learned normal manifold; the evaluation remains zero-shot without supervised fine-tuning or adversarial robustness testing; perplexity may misflag creative/technical/minority-specific legitimate language (fairness concern).

## Future Work
- Incorporate genuine streaming sensor data (operational logs, environmental telemetry, device usage traces) for full IoT–sensor fusion and real-time anomaly detection.
- Add fairness calibration so creative/technical/domain-specific language is not misclassified.
- Add data-cleaning and robust representation-learning (pre-filtering, iterative outlier removal) to reduce bias from residual anomalous data.
- Deploy in stream-processing/edge-device environments; add empirical runtime profiling (latency, throughput, GPU/CPU utilization, energy).
- Include supervised fine-tuning and adversarial robustness studies, and benchmark against large-scale detectors (GPT-4-based, RoBERTa-large) on larger labeled fake-review datasets.

## Interesting Ideas
- Unified hybrid anomaly score combining linguistic fluency (perplexity), structural regularity (reconstruction error), semantic drift, and metadata inconsistency: A(x) = αP(x) + βE(x) + γΔ(x) + δS(x).
- Semantic drift metric Δ = ||z_inst − h(x)|| comparing an instruction-conditioned embedding to an unsupervised autoencoder latent vector.
- Metadata anomaly components: temporal deviation (robust z-score with median/MAD), verification-prior deviation, rating–sentiment inconsistency, and category–text semantic misalignment (cosine to category centroid).
- Prompt-quality sensitivity study (generic vs. task-aligned prompts) showing 0.10–0.14 AUC gains with meaningful prompts.
- Calibrating synthetic anomalies to match real fake-review statistics (n-gram entropy, sentiment polarity, length).

## Possible Inspiration For Our Paper
- The zero-shot, label-free detection paradigm using instruction-tuned LLM perplexity + autoencoder reconstruction + semantic drift could be adapted to fake-news detection where labeled data is scarce.
- The hybrid weighted-fusion anomaly score and threshold-only calibration is a clean, reproducible baseline design for our benchmark.
- Metadata/context validation (temporal, source-verification, content–category consistency) maps naturally to fake-news signals (posting time, source credibility, headline–body consistency).
- Interpretability via token-level saliency (Grad/IG/LIME) is a template for explainable fake-news classifiers.
- Prompt-sensitivity analysis and few-shot scaling curves are useful evaluation protocols to include in the benchmark.

## Reviewer Notes
Most directly benchmark-relevant of the three papers: a primary, reproducible study of label-free fraud/anomaly detection on user reviews, with clear architecture, hyperparameters, ablations, and baselines transferable to fake-news detection. Caveats before reuse: the "multi-modal / sensor" framing is aspirational (metadata proxies, not real sensor data); anomalies are synthetic; and there are internal inconsistencies (hardware, PyTorch version, and headline AUC of 0.945/0.95 vs. table maxima ~0.933–0.935). Treat the ablation and cross-benchmark tables (Full Hybrid 0.918; cross-benchmark 0.918 AUC) as the most trustworthy figures, and verify the headline numbers against the tables when quoting.
