## Title
A systematic review of multimodal fake news detection on social media using deep learning models

## Authors
Maged Nasser; Noreen Izza Arshad; Abdulalem Ali; Hitham Alhussian; Faisal Saeed; Aminu Da'u; Ibtehal Nafea

## Year
2025

## Venue
Results in Engineering, Volume 26 (2025) 104752 (Elsevier / ScienceDirect). Received 14 December 2024; revised 13 March 2025; accepted 28 March 2025; available online 9 April 2025.

## DOI
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rineng.2025.104752

## Research Objective
"This paper provides a comprehensive review and analysis of existent DL-based models for multimodal fake news detection, focusing on diverse aspects, including user profiles, news content, images, videos, and audio data." The study is a systematic literature review (SLR) following PRISMA guidelines covering articles from 2018 to 2025.

## Research Questions
- RQ-1: What are the recent DL methods used for multi-modal fake news detection problems?
- RQ-2: What are the features commonly utilized in the multimodal fake news detection with DL models?
- RQ-3: What are the standard datasets commonly utilized in the literature for evaluation of the DL models for the fake news detection?
- RQ-4: What are the common performance metrics for fake news detection models using DL?
- RQ-5: What are the challenges and prospects of DL-based fake news detection approaches?

## Dataset(s)
This is a review paper; it does not introduce a dataset but surveys standard datasets used across the reviewed studies. Datasets described (Table 5 and Section 5):
- Text + image: Twitter (MediaEval-2015/2016), Weibo, PolitiFact (FakeNewsNet), GossipCop (FakeNewsNet), Fakeddit, FakeNewsNet, PHEME
- Text + video: VAVD (Volunteer Annotated Video Dataset), Fake Video Corpus (FVC), Misleading YouTube Video Corpus (MYVC)
- Audio + video: Deepfake Detection Challenge (DFDC), DeepFake-TIMIT, FakeAVCeleb, World Leaders Deepfake Dataset (WLDD), Presidential Deepfakes Dataset (PDD)
- Text + audio + video: FakeSV

## Dataset Size
Reported per dataset (F = fake, R = real, I = images, V = videos):
- Twitter (MediaEval-2015): training 5008 real and 6840 fake tweets; test 2564 fake and 1217 real. MediaEval-2016 test: 1107 real and 1121 false posts. (Table 5: 7032 F, 361 I, 5008 R)
- Weibo: original 9528 news items (4749 false, 4779 true), all with images; training 3749 fake / 3783 true; test 1000 fake / 996 true.
- PolitiFact: 1056 total (624 true, 432 false); 948 with text (528 true, 420 false); 783 with images (447 true, 336 false).
- GossipCop: 22,140 items (5323 fake, 16,817 true); 21,641 with text (16,694 authentic, 4947 fraudulent); 18,417 with images (16,767 authentic, 1650 fraudulent).
- Fakeddit: 1,063,106 news occurrences (binary, 3-way, and 6-way categories).
- VAVD: over 100,000 videos/comments (YouTube, Sept 2013–Oct 2016); Table 5: 100,000 V.
- FVC: latest version 2458 real and 3957 fraudulent videos (Table 5: 6515 V, 2458 R, 3957 F).
- MYVC: 902 fake and 903 real (1805 V).
- DFDC: training 119,154 10-second clips (100,000 deepfakes, 486 distinct individuals); validation 4000 clips (2000 deepfakes, 214 subjects); test 5000 deepfake clips.
- DeepFake-TIMIT: 43 people, 10 sentences each.
- FakeAVCeleb: 20,000 videos (500 real, 19,500 fake).
- WLDD: real portion 30,683 10-second films (1004 distinct individuals).
- PDD: 32 films (16 altered).
- FakeSV: 1827 fake and 1827 real.
- FakeNewsNet (Table 5): 5367 F, 19,200 I, 17,222 R.
- PHEME (Table 5): 1972 F, 2672 I, 3830 R.
Review corpus: over 1200 studies identified; 963 after duplicate/screening removal; 121 studies finally selected for the SLR.

## Language(s)
Reviewed articles limited to English-language publications. Datasets surveyed cover English (Twitter, PolitiFact, GossipCop, Fakeddit) and Chinese (Weibo, FakeSV from Douyin/Kuaishou); the paper also notes one MVAE study considered Portuguese and English. Paper explicitly notes most reviewed approaches focus on English-language fake news detection.

## Task
Systematic literature review of deep-learning-based multimodal fake news detection on social media. The underlying detection task in reviewed works is generally binary classification (fake vs. real), with some multiclass (e.g., Fakeddit 3-way/6-way, PHEME 3-class).

## Model(s)
Deep learning model families reviewed (Table 4): Attention Mechanism, Autoencoder (AE), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), Recursive Neural Networks (RvNN), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), Transformer (BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT), Boltzmann Machines, and Ensemble methods. Frequently referenced building blocks include BERT, VGG-16/VGG-19, ResNet-50, ViT, CapsNet, VGGish, EfficientNet-B0, Xception, Swin Transformer, Word2vec, GloVe, TF-IDF.

## Architecture Type
Review / survey paper (SLR). It does not propose a single architecture; it taxonomizes multimodal deep learning architectures (Transformer, RNN, CNN, RvNN, AE, GAN, GRU, Boltzmann Machine, Attention, Ensemble) and fusion methods (e.g., co-attention, multimodal bilinear pooling, cross-attention, transfer learning fusion). Finding: Transformer models and RNNs are the most popular, followed by CNNs.

## Preprocessing Pipeline
Described generically (Section 3.2.1): data pre-processing (managing missing words, binarizing attributes, expressing ambiguous attributes, handling complex structures); word vectorization; TF-IDF with bag-of-words; pre-trained embeddings such as word2vec and GloVe; N-gram generation; linguistic feature extraction (quantity features, user credibility, stylistic characteristics, psycho-linguistic features, readability index); word embedding via BERT for text; image feature extraction commonly via VGG-19 with image size normalization and train/test splitting; textual and visual data pre-processed individually then merged (title, text, vision).

## Training Strategy
Not Reported (review paper; describes strategies used in surveyed works such as transfer learning, contrastive learning, adversarial learning, multitask learning, and one-class learning, but no unified training procedure of its own).

## Hyperparameters
Not Reported

## Evaluation Metrics
Metrics surveyed (Table 6): Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, Cross Entropy Loss, AUC-ROC Curve (True Positive Rate / False Positive Rate), and Computational Cost (FLOPs × Time × Energy). Confusion matrix (true positives, true negatives, false positives, false negatives) discussed. Finding: accuracy, precision, recall, and F-scores are the most frequently used metrics.

## Baseline Models
Not Reported (review paper; no experimental baselines of its own).

## Main Results
Key SLR findings:
- Transformer models and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are the most popular DL techniques for multimodal fake news detection, followed by CNNs.
- Twitter and Weibo datasets are the two most frequently used standard datasets.
- Accuracy, precision, recall, and F-scores are the most frequently used evaluation metrics.
- 121 studies were selected (from 963 quality articles / over 1200 initially identified) after screening and eligibility analysis.
- Publications per year among selected studies: 2020 = 12, 2021 = 13, 2023 = 21, 2024 = 23 (rising trend from 2019).
- Number of approaches used per year: 14 in 2018, 20 in 2019, 28 in 2021, 32 in 2022, 46 in 2023 (Jan–Aug).
Specific results quoted from reviewed studies: UCNet achieved a macro-averaged F-score of 0.82 on VAVD and FVC; a coupled matrix-tensor factorization deep neural network achieved a validation precision/accuracy of 92.30% on BuzzFeed and PolitiFact.

## Strengths
- Comprehensive, PRISMA-based systematic methodology with explicit search strings, inclusion/exclusion criteria (Table 1), and a standard quality checklist (Table 2).
- Broad coverage across modality combinations (text+image, text+video, audio+video, text+audio+video, user profile + content).
- Provides consolidated taxonomies of features, model families with advantages/disadvantages (Table 4), datasets with statistics (Table 5), and evaluation metrics with formulas (Table 6).
- Recent coverage window (2018–2025).

## Weaknesses
- As a review, it aggregates reported numbers from primary studies rather than performing controlled, comparable experiments; reported accuracies come from heterogeneous settings.
- Some dataset statistics in Table 5 appear inconsistent/duplicated (e.g., identical F/I/R values repeated for Weibo, PolitiFact, and GossipCop rows), reducing reliability of that table.
- Heavy reliance on paraphrased descriptions; several passages contain garbled/typo-laden text.
- No quantitative meta-analysis or statistical synthesis of performance across studies.

## Limitations
Stated by the authors (Section 7):
- The SLR only includes publications from journals and conferences describing DL-based fake news detection methods; other sources (e.g., additional sourcebooks) were excluded and could have enhanced the review.
- Search restricted to English-language works only, which could introduce linguistic bias.
- Although principal databases were used, other digital libraries with pertinent studies may have been overlooked, and certain keyword synonyms may have been missed.

## Future Work
Stated by the authors (abstract, Sections 8 and 9):
- Designing robust multilingual and cross-lingual fake news detection systems (current approaches mostly rely on English).
- Hybridization of deep learning models and exploration of transformer models to enhance detection accuracy.
- Integration of explainable AI (XAI).
- Facilitating real-time fake news detection models.
- Early detection of fake news (semi-/weakly-supervised settings with few or no labels).
- Addressing varying degrees of falseness (multiclass rather than binary).
- Better handling of multimodality and relationships among modalities.
- Greater availability of (multimodal) datasets for training and testing.

## Interesting Ideas
- Taxonomy of fusion methods (co-attention, multimodal bilinear/factorized bilinear pooling, cross-attention, relationship-aware attention, mutual knowledge distillation, gated VAE for noise suppression).
- Emotion-aided multitask framework (fake news detection + emotion recognition) with supervised contrastive learning (FakeClips).
- Quantum multimodal fusion (QMFND) using a quantum convolutional neural network.
- Modality Dissonance Score (MDS) and audio-visual affective cues for deepfake detection.
- One-class learning with multimodal variational autoencoders for label-scarce settings.

## Possible Inspiration For Our Paper
- Use its dataset catalog (Table 5) as a shortlist of benchmark datasets for our fake-news benchmark: Twitter (MediaEval), Weibo, PolitiFact/GossipCop (FakeNewsNet), Fakeddit, PHEME, FakeSV, FakeAVCeleb.
- Adopt the standard metric set (accuracy, precision, recall, F1; plus AUC-ROC and computational cost) for consistent benchmark reporting.
- Motivates including multilingual and multimodal test conditions and an explainability (XAI) dimension in the benchmark.
- The model-family taxonomy (Transformer/RNN/CNN/AE/GAN/GRU/Ensemble) can structure which architecture classes our benchmark should represent.

## Potential Citation Usage
- Cite as a recent (2025) systematic survey establishing that Transformers and RNNs dominate multimodal fake news detection and that Twitter/Weibo are the most-used benchmarks — useful for framing/related-work and dataset-selection justification.
- Cite for evidence that datasets are scarce/small and English-centric, motivating a new benchmark.
- Cite for the standard evaluation-metric conventions in the field.

## Reviewer Notes
Strong scoping source for the benchmark project: it enumerates datasets, modalities, model families, fusion strategies, and metrics in one place. Treat its aggregated performance numbers (e.g., 92.30%, F-score 0.82) as secondary (originating from primary studies, non-comparable settings), and verify Table 5 statistics against original dataset papers before reuse, since several rows appear duplicated/erroneous. Best used for related-work framing, dataset shortlisting, and metric standardization rather than as a source of primary experimental results.
