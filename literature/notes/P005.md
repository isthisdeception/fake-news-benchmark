## Paper ID
P005

## Title
Balanced Multi-modal Learning with Hierarchical Fusion for Fake News Detection

## Authors
Fei Wu, Shu Chen, Guangwei Gao, Yimu Ji, Xiao-Yuan Jing

## Year
2025

## Venue
Pattern Recognition, Volume 164 (2025) 111485 (Elsevier)

## DOI
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2025.111485

## Research Objective
To address the modality imbalance problem in multi-modal fake news detection (MFND), where the dominant modality suppresses the performance of other modalities during optimization, and to guarantee effective utilization of each modality. The authors propose Balanced Multi-modal Learning with Hierarchical Fusion (BMLHF), containing a Multi-modal Information Balancing (MIB) module and a Hierarchical Fusion (HF) module, to solve modality imbalance and improve the discriminability of multi-modal fused features.

## Research Questions
Not Reported (no explicitly enumerated research questions; framed as contributions). Stated main contributions:
(1) A Multi-modal Information Balancing (MIB) module that calculates modal information, obtains the modal difference ratio, and dynamically allocates weights to different views of modalities during optimization to achieve modal information balance (described as a relatively early work investigating modality imbalance in MFND).
(2) A Hierarchical Fusion (HF) module performing cross-modal and cross-view fusion, including a dual cross-transformer interaction block and two fusion stages (intra-modal multi-view information fusion and inter-modal correlation fusion).
(3) Experimental results on Twitter, Weibo, and Fakeddit demonstrating the approach outperforms state-of-the-art MFND methods by a large margin.

## Dataset(s)
Twitter [1] (MediaEval Verifying Multimedia Use benchmark), Weibo [2] (collected by Jin), and Fakeddit [29] (r/fakeddit, created by research team at University of California).

## Dataset Size
- Weibo: training set 7532 news items, testing set 1996 news items (split ratio 8:2).
- Twitter: training set 8617 news items, testing set 2059 news items.
- Fakeddit: contains over one million samples classified into six categories; following [51], 30,000 image–text pairs selected for training and 10,000 image–text pairs for testing.

## Language(s)
Not Reported explicitly per dataset (Weibo is Chinese-language, Twitter/Fakeddit English-language, but the paper does not state languages directly)

## Task
Multi-modal fake news detection (MFND) — binary classification of news (fake vs real) using text and image modalities.

## Model(s)
BMLHF (proposed), composed of:
- Feature extractors (four channels): BERT (text pattern features), Swin-T (image pattern features), CLIP-text (text semantic features), CLIP-image (image semantic features).
- MIB module (dynamic gradient weighting via balance factors).
- HF module: dual cross-transformer interaction block (pattern and semantic cross-transformers) + two-stage fusion (intra-modal multi-view attention fusion; inter-modal joint correlation matrix based cross-attention fusion).
- Fake news classifier: one-layer MLP with ReLU activation.

## Architecture Type
Multi-modal deep learning architecture with transformer-based feature extractors (BERT, Swin Transformer, CLIP), cross-modal transformers, attention-based hierarchical fusion, and gradient-modulation-based balanced multi-modal learning.

## Preprocessing Pipeline
Not Reported as an explicit text-cleaning pipeline. Feature extraction settings: textual token embeddings obtained by pre-trained BERT-base with embedding dimension 768; visual representation dimension set to 1000; four types of extracted features projected to the same dimension via a two-layer MLP (hidden layer dimension 384 with ReLU).

## Training Strategy
- Weibo split into training/testing at ratio 8:2; Twitter and Fakeddit use fixed train/test splits as above.
- Adam optimizer used to train the model with weight decay of 0.001.
- Total loss L_total = L_c + α2·L_s, where L_c is cross-entropy loss and L_s is a cross-modal supervised contrastive loss.
- MIB adjusts each modality's gradient adaptively using balance factors k^u_h based on difference rate ρ^u_h (inhibiting optimization of views with ρ^u_h > 1/4).
- Grid search used to fine-tune hyperparameters.
- Maximum training epoch number set to 100; best result achieved at about 70 epochs.

## Hyperparameters
- Learning rate: fixed to 0.001 for all three datasets.
- Optimizer: Adam, weight decay 0.001.
- Maximum training epochs: 100.
- BERT embedding dimension: 768; visual representation dimension: 1000; MLP hidden layer dimension: 384 (ReLU).
- Number of heads of cross-transformer block: 4.
- τ (contrastive loss temperature scalar): 0.5 (empirically set; better in range [0.3,0.5] on Weibo, 0.5 on Twitter/Fakeddit).
- α1: 0.8 (better in range [0.7,0.9] on Weibo, 0.8 on Twitter/Fakeddit).
- α2 (balance factor for L_s): 0.5 (better in range [0.4,0.6] on Weibo, 0.5 on Twitter/Fakeddit).
- Search range for τ, α1, α2: [0,1].

## Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy (Acc), Precision (Pre), Recall (Rec), and F1 (reported separately for fake news and real news). Kruskal–Wallis statistical significance test at significance level 0.05 (P-values). Computational cost: training time per epoch, testing time on total test set, and number of parameters (M).

## Baseline Models
Uni-modal: BERT [48], Swin-T [49].
Multi-modal: EANN [17], SpotFake+ [18], SAFE [19], CAFE [20], MRML [21], LogicDM [22], BMR [23], QMFND [46], MTTV [52].

## Main Results
Overall performance (Table 1, Accuracy and F1 for fake/real):
- Weibo: BMLHF Acc 0.912, Fake F1 0.903, Real F1 0.902 (best prior: MRML Acc 0.897). Accuracy improvement 0.015 over MRML on Weibo.
- Twitter: BMLHF Acc 0.966, Fake F1 0.957, Real F1 0.956 (best prior: QMFND Acc 0.918). Accuracy improvement 0.048 on Twitter.
- Fakeddit: BMLHF Acc 0.950, Fake F1 0.950, Real F1 0.950 (best prior: QMFND Acc 0.942). Accuracy improvement 0.008 on Fakeddit.
- F1 (fake) improvements: 0.002 (Weibo), 0.022 (Twitter), 0.010 (Fakeddit). F1 (real) improvements: 0.010 (Weibo), 0.046 (Twitter), 0.010 (Fakeddit).

Selected BMLHF detailed values (Table 1):
- Weibo: Acc 0.912±0.009; Fake Pre 0.930, Rec 0.880, F1 0.903; Real Pre 0.894, Rec 0.920, F1 0.902.
- Twitter: Acc 0.966±0.013; Fake Pre 0.985, Rec 0.933, F1 0.957; Real Pre 0.948, Rec 0.975, F1 0.956.
- Fakeddit: Acc 0.950±0.012; Fake Pre 0.945, Rec 0.955, F1 0.950; Real Pre 0.955, Rec 0.945, F1 0.950.

Ablation (Table 2), accuracy drops from full BMLHF:
- BMLHF-I (remove MIB): Weibo 0.874 (−0.038), Twitter 0.926 (−0.040).
- BMLHF-T (remove two-stage fusion of HF): Weibo 0.890 (−0.022), Twitter 0.942 (−0.024).
- BMLHF-D (remove dual cross-transformer): Weibo 0.873 (−0.039), Twitter 0.925 (−0.041).
- BMLHF-H (remove whole HF): Weibo 0.868 (−0.044), Twitter 0.910 (−0.056), Fakeddit 0.920 (−0.030).
- BMLHF-tra (remove first fusion stage): Weibo 0.902 (−0.010), Twitter 0.933 (−0.033), Fakeddit 0.935 (−0.015).
- BMLHF-ter (remove second fusion stage): Weibo 0.893 (−0.019), Twitter 0.930 (−0.036), Fakeddit 0.936 (−0.014).

Statistical significance: all P-values against compared methods significantly lower than 0.05.
Computational cost (Table 4): BMLHF parameters 14.85 M (vs MRML 13.55 M, LogicDM 18.05 M); training/testing times comparable to MRML and LogicDM.

## Strengths
- Directly targets a comparatively under-explored problem (modality imbalance) in MFND, adapting ideas from audio-visual imbalanced learning.
- Consistent, large improvements across three benchmarks (Weibo, Twitter, Fakeddit) on comprehensive metrics.
- Thorough ablation isolating MIB, dual cross-transformer, and each fusion stage.
- Statistical significance testing (Kruskal–Wallis) and computational cost analysis included.
- Uses four complementary views (pattern via BERT/Swin-T; semantic via CLIP-text/CLIP-image), plus supervised contrastive loss for discriminability.
- Shows MIB is plug-and-play: applying it to EANN, MRML, LogicDM improves their accuracy.

## Weaknesses
- Limited to text and image modalities; video and audio not handled (acknowledged).
- No explicit text/image preprocessing pipeline reported, reducing reproducibility.
- Research questions not explicitly framed; some equations in the PDF appear with formatting/typographical issues (e.g., balance factor definition, Algorithm 1 step 3).
- Datasets are established benchmarks that may be saturated; cross-dataset generalization not evaluated.
- Languages per dataset not explicitly stated.

## Limitations
Stated by the authors (Conclusion): "Currently, our approach mainly focuses on image and text modalities." The model has not yet been evaluated on more diverse modalities such as video, which is needed to demonstrate generalization and determine reliability in more complex practical application scenarios.

## Future Work
Stated by the authors (Conclusion): BMLHF will be further evaluated on more real-world experimental data with diverse modalities, e.g., video, to demonstrate its generalization and help determine the reliability of the model in more complex practical application scenarios.

## Interesting Ideas
- Using logit scores as a proxy for per-modality "information" and computing a difference rate ρ to detect and correct modality imbalance during training.
- Balance factor k^u_h = 1 − tanh(α1·ρ^u_h) applied only to over-dominant views (ρ > 1/4) to modulate gradient updates.
- Four-view feature design separating "pattern" features (BERT/Swin-T) from "semantic" features (CLIP), fused hierarchically.
- Joint correlation matrix based cross-attention (G_I, G_T via tanh(X L^T / √d)) capturing intra- and inter-modal correlations.
- Cross-modal supervised contrastive loss to improve feature discriminability.
- MIB demonstrated as a transferable plug-in module improving other MFND models.

## Possible Inspiration For Our Paper
- Modality imbalance as an evaluation axis: a fake-news benchmark could measure per-modality contribution/logit imbalance, not just overall accuracy.
- The MIB gradient-modulation idea could be a benchmarked training strategy across multi-modal baselines.
- Reporting fake-class and real-class F1 separately (rather than a single averaged F1) is a useful benchmark reporting practice.
- Twitter/Weibo/Fakeddit splits and sizes provide concrete, reusable benchmark configurations.
- Including significance testing and computational cost (params, train/test time) is a good multi-dimensional benchmark template.

## Potential Citation Usage
- Cite as SOTA multi-modal (text+image) fake news detection baseline with strong Twitter/Weibo/Fakeddit numbers.
- Cite for the modality imbalance problem framing and the observation that text modality dominates image modality in MFND.
- Cite for the use of CLIP + BERT + Swin-T as complementary multi-view extractors.
- Cite its reported baseline numbers (EANN, SpotFake+, SAFE, CAFE, MRML, LogicDM, BMR, QMFND, MTTV) as reference values on these datasets.

## Reviewer Notes

- Some reported numbers show BMLHF not best on every single sub-metric (e.g., Twitter fake Recall 0.933 vs QMFND 0.970), but best on aggregate/accuracy — consistent with the paper's own caveat that competitors can win on isolated metrics.
- Equation/OCR artifacts in the extracted PDF (balance-factor piecewise definition, Algorithm 1 line 3) should be verified against the publisher version before quoting formulas.
- Good candidate as a required multi-modal baseline in the benchmark; note the text+image-only scope.
