## Research Objective
To act as a fundamental resource for researchers beginning their exploration into fake news detection by emphasizing the importance of dataset quality and diversity in enhancing detection model effectiveness. The survey investigates the key components of datasets used in developing fake news detection models — their characteristics, common features, labels, prevalent biases, challenges, limitations, ethical considerations, and best practices — and contributes a GitHub repository aggregating publicly available datasets into a single accessible portal.

## Research Questions
Not Reported (framed as a systematic review with objectives/section aims rather than enumerated research questions). The survey aims to examine: how dataset characteristics influence performance and effectiveness of fake news detection models; characteristics, common features, and labels of existing datasets; the role of multimodal datasets; challenges, biases, and ethical considerations; best practices for constructing high-quality datasets; and the evolution of detection models with dataset availability.

## Main Results
Key synthesized findings:
- Dataset size and diversity strongly influence detection performance; larger datasets (e.g., NELA-GT-2018, ~713 K articles) improve generalization (up to 15% accuracy improvement reported vs smaller datasets); smaller datasets risk overfitting (10–20% accuracy drop on unseen data).
- Multimodal datasets generally outperform unimodal ones (Fakeddit + CNN achieved 87% accuracy; multimodal can improve accuracy by up to 8.11% over text-only).
- Cross-dataset generalization is a central bottleneck: non-trivial degradation (moderate ~8–15% to severe >40%) even with identical architectures (Table 5: Li et al. 2024 Weak on DGM4; Rout et al. 2025 Moderate on FakeNewsNet/ISOT/LIAR; Aslam et al. 2025 Moderate on multiple benchmarks; Xie et al. 2025 Strong, transfer-focused, on Twitter/Weibo).
- Benchmark saturation/aging: legacy benchmarks such as ISOT show near-perfect (>99%) accuracy but degrade on harder datasets (LIAR, FakeNewsNet).
- Biases quantified from cited works: MediaEval-type dataset ~60% political vs ~10% health news → 85% accuracy on political but 60% on health (Baly et al.); FakeNewsNet over-represents popular sources → 78% on well-known vs 55% on less prominent sources (Shu et al.); annotator political alignment → articles aligned with annotators' views 30% less likely labeled fake; stricter guidelines increased inter-annotator agreement by 15% (Chen et al.).
- LLM-era: LLM-generated misinformation is fluent and stylistically indistinguishable from human text, and static benchmarks suffer contamination, undermining reproducibility.
- Contribution: a GitHub repository aggregating publicly available datasets (https://github.com/fakenewsresearch/dataset).

## Strengths
- Dataset-centric perspective distinct from prior method-centric surveys; consolidates dataset characteristics, sizes, labels, rating scales, and update status in comparative tables.
- Follows PRISMA 2020 with a documented search strategy, flow diagram, and checklist (Appendix B), improving transparency/reproducibility.
- Bibliometric analysis via VOSviewer surfaces dominant themes.
- Covers timely topics: cross-dataset generalization, LLM-era benchmark instability/contamination, and ethical considerations (privacy, consent, societal impact, transparency).
- Provides a maintained GitHub portal aggregating datasets.

## Weaknesses
- Reports specific quantitative claims (e.g., accuracy percentages, bias figures) sourced from cited works rather than reproduced, so numbers should be traced to originals.
- Some internal inconsistency in reported counts (initial search "134" in figure vs "125" in text; "102" vs "93" unique/included articles).
- Table 3 rating scales for r/fakeddit and M4 are listed identically to CREDBANK's 5-value credibility scale, which appears inconsistent with those datasets' actual label schemes.
- No new empirical evaluation or benchmark is produced; conclusions are synthesized from heterogeneous studies with differing metrics.
- Search limited to 2023 onward (for Scopus) may under-cover foundational dataset papers except via supplementary sources.

## Limitations

## Future Work
Stated by the authors (Section 11, Future Directions): (1) develop adaptive/dynamic datasets regularly updated from social media, news websites, and forums, with model feedback loops and anomaly detection to incorporate emerging misinformation patterns; (2) use synthetic data generation (e.g., GANs) to supplement real data and address scarcity/imbalance, combining synthetic and real data via augmentation/transfer learning with bias detection and correction (fairness-aware objectives, auditing); (3) develop multilingual and cross-cultural models via multilingual corpora, cross-lingual transfer learning, and culturally aware embeddings/annotations/evaluation; (4) build continuously updated, multimodal datasets for detecting AI-generated fake news from modern LLMs and diffusion-based image generators.

## Interesting Ideas
- "It's all in the data": reframing detection progress as fundamentally dataset-driven rather than architecture-driven.
- Systematic distinction between within-dataset vs cross-dataset evaluation, and the claim that cross-dataset generalization is constrained by dataset design more than model capacity.
- Benchmark aging/saturation (ISOT near-perfect accuracy) and data contamination in the LLM era as reproducibility threats.
- Cataloguing datasets by data type symbols (text, images, video, metadata, generated text) and rating scales, plus "regularly updated" status (Table 6).
- Ethical framing: privacy/consent, bias propagation, transparency/accountability, and journalism/cross-cultural implications of dataset design.
- POLygraph's question-and-answer annotation scheme (intention, target, potential harm) beyond simple factuality.
