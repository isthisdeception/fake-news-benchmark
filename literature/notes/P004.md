## Title
GETAE: Graph Information Enhanced Deep Neural NeTwork Ensemble ArchitecturE for fake news detection

## Authors
Ciprian-Octavian Truică, Elena-Simona Apostol, Marius Marogel, Adrian Paschke

## Year
2025

## Venue
Expert Systems With Applications, Volume 275 (2025) 126984 (Elsevier)

## DOI
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.126984

## Research Objective
"The main objective of this work is to design and implement the novel Graph Information Enhanced Deep Neural Network Ensemble ArchitecturE for Fake News Detection, i.e., GETAE. GETAE uses textual content and social interactions to improve fake news detection."

Secondary objectives (fivefold as stated):
(1) Design a novel Text Content Embedding combining lexical (character and word level) and syntactic (sentence-level) textual features.
(2) Create a new Propagation Embedding designed to encode information diffusion (spread from a node to its followers).
(3) Propose an innovative Propagation-Enhanced Content Embedding encapsulating textual content, contextual data, and propagation information.
(4) Benchmark GETAE by training multiple models using cross-validation, ablation testing, and hyperparameter tuning on Twitter15 and Twitter16.
(5) Compare the best GETAE models with current state-of-the-art models.

## Research Questions
- (Q1) How much dataset-reliant is the choice of models and algorithms for Fake News Detection?
- (Q2) What is the impact of word embeddings that combine multiple complex textual features such as lexical (e.g., character and word level features) and syntactic (e.g., sentence-level features), on the performance of Fake News Detection systems?
- (Q3) What is the impact of employing node embeddings that encode the information diffusion through social networks on the effectiveness of Fake News Detection methods?
- (Q4) What is the impact of embeddings that consider both the information diffusion and the textual content and context on the performance of Fake News Detection systems?
- (Q5) How are these models performing in comparison with state-of-the-art Fake News Detection models?

## Dataset(s)
Twitter15 and Twitter16 (Ma, Gao and Wong, 2017). Both contain: (1) source tweets with tweet ID and text content, (2) label assigning a class to each tweet ID, and (3) the propagation tree of each tweet. Both datasets are labeled using 4 classes: fake, true, non-rumor, and unverified. Only source tweets labeled as true and fake were used (binary classification).

## Dataset Size
- Twitter15: total number of graphs is 742 (stated in network data analysis). Datasets described as "fairly balanced" for the true/fake classes; exact per-class counts Not Reported in text.
- Tweet characteristics: most probable length around 80–100 characters; most tweets in 10–15 words range with maximum word count under 30 words.
- Twitter16: exact number of graphs/samples Not Reported (described with similar degree distribution to Twitter15).

## Language(s)
Not Reported (datasets are Twitter data; language not explicitly stated, though English-oriented tools like BERTweet were used)

## Task
Binary fake news detection / classification (determining veracity of a post: true vs fake) on social media.

## Model(s)
GETAE ensemble architecture, consisting of:
- Text Branch: Word Embedding layer (Word2Vec Skip-Gram, BERT, or BERTweet) + [Bi]RNN hidden layer (RNN, BiRNN, GRU, BiGRU, LSTM, or BiLSTM).
- Propagation Branch: Node Embedding layer (Node2Vec or DeepWalk) + Dense (ReLU) layer.
- Ensemble: concatenation producing Propagation-Enhanced Content Embedding, then Dense (ReLU) layer + output Dense (softmax/sigmoid).
Word embeddings: Word2Vec Skip-Gram (trained), BERT ('bert-base-uncased'), BERTweet ('vinai/bertweet-base'). Node embeddings: Node2Vec, DeepWalk.

## Architecture Type
Deep Neural Network Ensemble Architecture combining feed-forward and bidirectional Recurrent Neural Networks ([Bi]RNN) for text with graph node embeddings (Node2Vec/DeepWalk) for propagation; two-branch (Text Branch + Propagation Branch) ensemble.

## Preprocessing Pipeline
Text preprocessing steps applied: (1) remove URL; (2) remove punctuation; (3) remove stopwords (only for the Word2Vec model); (4) assign each token a unique integer identifier (id) starting with id 1. Text reconstructed into a 2-dimensional document-to-token matrix D ∈ N^(n×k) with left zero padding for documents smaller than maximum size k. Implemented in Python 3.10 using nltk. For experimental setup, text preprocessing for training word embeddings consisted of removing (1) URLs, (2) double spaces, and (3) punctuations.
Graph: users as nodes, posting/interaction (re-sharing, linking, commenting) as edges; extract information diffusion propagation graph. Node embeddings via node2vec and karateclub packages.

## Training Strategy
- 80–20 train–test ratio.
- Models trained for 30 epochs, except Word2Vec-based models trained for only 8 epochs to avoid overfitting.
- Learning rate of 0.001 for training the GETAE models.
- k-fold cross-validation with k = 10 (with 80-20 train/test ratio); Stratified K-Fold validation; 10 experiments per measure with randomized train–test-validation split each time.
- Tables present mean and standard deviation per metric.
- Training on Google Colab, with no hardware acceleration.
- Implemented with TensorFlow and Keras. Code at https://github.com/DS4AI-UPB/GETAE.

## Hyperparameters
Embeddings hyperparameters (Table 1):
- Word2Vec: Window 10, Min count 4, Learning rate 0.025, Epochs 5.
- Node2Vec: Window 10, Min count 1, Walk length 10, Learning rate 0.05, Epochs 1.
- DeepWalk: Window 5, Min count 1, Walk length 10, Learning rate 0.05, Epochs 1.

BERT tokenizer hyperparameters (Table 2): add_special_tokens TRUE, Padding TRUE, Truncation TRUE, max_length 128 (same for BERT and BERTweet).

GETAE layers hyperparameters (Table 3):
- RNN: 64 units, Dropout 0.2, Tanh.
- BiRNN: 128 units, Dropout 0.2, Tanh.
- GRU: 64 units, Dropout 0.2, Tanh.
- BiGRU: 128 units, Dropout 0.2, Tanh.
- LSTM: 64 units, Dropout 0.2, Tanh.
- BiLSTM: 128 units, Dropout 0.2, Tanh.
- Text dense: 32 units, ReLU.
- Graph dense: 32 units, ReLU.
- Output dense: 1 unit, Sigmoid.

Node embedding hyperparameters (main results): Node2Vec p = 1, q = 1, d = 100; DeepWalk d = 100. Training learning rate 0.001; 30 epochs (8 for Word2Vec). Tuning ranges explored: p, q ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}, d ∈ {32, 100}.

## Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score.

## Baseline Models
DTC (Castillo et al., 2011); SVM-TS (Ma et al., 2016); mGRU (Ma et al., 2016); RFC (Kwon et al., 2017); tCNN (Yang et al., 2018); CRNN (Liu & Wu, 2018); CSI (Ruchansky et al., 2017); dEFEND (Shu et al., 2019); DANES (Truică, Apostol et al., 2024); GCAN-G (Lu & Li, 2020); GCAN (Lu & Li, 2020). For node-embedding comparison: HiMaP (Mishra, 2020) and SSGE (Vu & Jung, 2021).

## Main Results
Best GETAE configurations:
- Twitter15: best by metrics is a tie between (BERT, Node2Vec, BiLSTM) and (BERTweet, DeepWalk, BiRNN); (BERT, Node2Vec, BiLSTM) chosen for higher F1. Reported: Accuracy 0.827, Precision 0.831, Recall 0.827, F1-Score 0.825 (from Table 6; comparison table lists GETAE Twitter15 Accuracy 0.825, Precision 0.827, Recall 0.831, F1 0.827).
- Twitter16: best configuration (BERT, Node2Vec, RNN) with 89.6% Accuracy, 90.1% Precision, 89.7% Recall, 89.5% F1-Score (comparison table lists GETAE Twitter16 Accuracy 0.895, Precision 0.897, Recall 0.901, F1 0.896).

Comparison with state-of-the-art (Table 10):
- Twitter15: GCAN Accuracy 0.8767 (highest accuracy), GCAN-G 0.8636, DANES 0.7790; GETAE 0.825 Accuracy, 0.827 Precision, 0.831 Recall, 0.827 F1. GETAE outperforms others in Precision/Recall/F1 but GCAN/GCAN-G have higher accuracy on Twitter15.
- Twitter16: GCAN Accuracy 0.9084 (highest accuracy); GETAE Accuracy 0.895, Precision 0.897, Recall 0.901, F1 0.896 — GETAE outperforms in Precision, Recall, F1.

Node-embedding comparison (Table 11):
- Twitter15 accuracy: HiMaP (Node2Vec) 0.846, HiMaP (DeepWalk) 0.825, SSGE (Node2Vec) 0.600, SSGE (DeepWalk) 0.500, GETAE (Node2Vec) 0.827, GETAE (DeepWalk) 0.814. GETAE slightly surpassed by HiMaP on Twitter15 (~1.9% accuracy).
- Twitter16 accuracy: HiMaP (Node2Vec) 0.867, HiMaP (DeepWalk) 0.850, SSGE (Node2Vec) 0.600, SSGE (DeepWalk) 0.500, GETAE (Node2Vec) 0.896, GETAE (DeepWalk) 0.881. GETAE obtains overall best on Twitter16.

Sensitivity: dimensions (d) hyperparameter had the most significant impact, followed by return parameter (p), with in–out parameter (q) least influential.

## Strengths
- Novel integration of information diffusion/propagation (graph node embeddings) with textual content in an ensemble, addressing a stated gap where few methods incorporate information propagation.
- Extensive experimentation: ablation testing (adding/removing Propagation Branch, changing RNN layer type), 10-fold cross-validation, and hyperparameter tuning; results reported with mean ± standard deviation.
- Modular architecture: any word/node embedding can be substituted.
- Publicly available code (GitHub) supporting reproducibility.
- Outperforms strong SOTA baselines (GCAN, DANES) in Precision/Recall/F1 on both datasets.

## Weaknesses
- Best accuracy on Twitter15 remains below GCAN (GETAE 0.825 vs GCAN 0.8767) and below HiMaP for node embeddings (0.846 vs 0.827); GETAE does not win on all metrics.
- Only binary classification used (true/fake), discarding non-rumor and unverified classes despite 4-class labels being available.
- Evaluated only on two relatively small Twitter datasets (Twitter15/Twitter16); generalization to other platforms/domains untested.
- Training done on Google Colab with no hardware acceleration; scalability to large datasets not demonstrated.

## Limitations
Stated by the authors (Discussion and limitations, Section 5): No single solution can fully address the multifaceted nature of fake news; the answer to whether the architecture is a definitive solution is "a qualified no." Identified primary challenges from the literature review: (1) need for stable datasets that preserve all metadata rather than relying on unstable URLs and unique IDs; (2) need for multi-modal datasets to train richer feature representations; (3) need for human-verified datasets to enhance feature selection. A significant limitation is the absence of large, annotated datasets capturing social media content and network characteristics (post reactions, diffusion, etc.); further shortcomings include veracity of data and how uncertainties/inaccuracies in data are learned by trained models.

## Future Work
Stated by the authors (Section 6): (1) incorporate additional context-based features such as number of likes, retweets, and comments; (2) add new convolutional layers to GETAE to better understand topological patterns of source nodes spreading fake news; (3) experiment on larger datasets and other social networks, creating domain-specific lexicon techniques for misinformation and propaganda detection and mitigation; (4) create a Mixture of Experts (MoE)-based solution for fake news detection; (5) investigate multimodal features (network, textual, visual, and potentially audio cues) enhanced with network information.

## Interesting Ideas
- Propagation-Enhanced Content Embedding: concatenating a Text Content Embedding with a Propagation Embedding into one representation for classification.
- Using Node2Vec/DeepWalk random-walk node embeddings to encode information diffusion in social graphs for detection.
- Modular ablation across six recurrent unit types (RNN/BiRNN/GRU/BiGRU/LSTM/BiLSTM) crossed with three word embeddings and two node embeddings.
- Node2Vec sensitivity analysis: dimensions matter most, then return parameter p, then in–out parameter q.
- Suggestion to integrate GETAE into network immunization (SparseShield, CONTAIN, MCWDST) and real-time detection (StopHC, ContCommRTD) pipelines.

- Reporting mean ± std over 10-fold stratified CV is a good rigor standard to adopt in a benchmark.
- Highlights that best model configuration is dataset-dependent (Q1) — motivating per-dataset tuning in a benchmark.
