## Title
Ensemble Transformer–Based Detection of Fake and AI–Generated News

## Authors
Md. Ishraquzzaman, Mohammed Ashraful Islam Chowdhury, Shahreen Rahman, and Riasat Khan (Electrical and Computer Engineering, North South University, Dhaka, Bangladesh)

## DOI

## Research Objective
To effectively detect fake and AI–generated news content by leveraging advanced natural language processing, machine learning, and deep learning algorithms; to create a first-of-its-kind dataset containing three types of news content (fake, real, and AI–generated), and to develop an optimized transformer-based ensemble model for evaluation. The stated aim is to "enhance information integrity and create a trusting, rational and well-informed society."

## Research Questions
Not Reported (no explicitly stated research questions; the paper lists contributions rather than formal RQs).

## Dataset(s)
Combined dataset (referred to as D6) built from five open-source datasets plus AI-generated content:
- FakeNewsNet (D1) [PolitiFact and GossipCop]
- COVID-19 Fake News Dataset (D2)
- LIAR (D3)
- ISOT (D4)
- TurningBench Dataset (D5) — AI–generated news
- Ensemble LLM–generated news articles (added to D6) generated by combining GPT-2, GPT-NEO, and Distil-GPT-2 with majority voting (10K unique news articles generated)

## Dataset Size
Combined dataset D6: 155,121 news titles — 43,372 true/real, 31,340 fake, and 80,409 AI–generated. (Abstract rounds to 43,000 real, 31,000 fake, and 80,000 AI–generated.)
Per-dataset breakdown (Table 1):
- D1 (FakeNewsNet): 17,441 real; 5,755 fake; total 23,196
- D2 (COVID-19): 2,061 real; 1,057 fake; total 3,118
- D3 (LIAR): 2,453 real; 1,047 fake; total 3,500 (after preprocessing)
- D4 (ISOT): 21,417 real; 23,481 fake; total 44,898
- D5 (TurningBench): 70,409 AI–generated; total 70,409
- D6 (Combined): 43,372 real; 31,340 fake; 80,409 AI–generated; total 155,121
Validation dataset size n = 120,000 (used in CI-based ensemble weighting).

## Language(s)
English

## Task
Multiclass news content classification into three categories: fake (label 0), real (label 1), and AI–generated (label 2). Also includes AI news title generation via an ensemble LLM.

## Model(s)
- Machine learning models (6): Naive Bayes, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression, XGBoost, AdaBoost
- NLP/deep learning models (5): BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, DistilBERT, XLNet
- Proposed Ensemble Transformer model: RoBERTa + DeBERTa (CI-based weighted voting)
- Ensemble LLM for text generation: GPT-2 + GPT-NEO + Distil-GPT-2 (majority voting)
- DistilBERT-Student model (knowledge distillation from RoBERTa-base teacher)

## Architecture Type
Transformer-based ensemble (RoBERTa + DeBERTa) with confidence-interval-based weighted voting; plus classical ML models with TF-IDF features; plus ensemble generative LLM (decoder-based GPT family models); knowledge distillation (feature- and logit-based).

## Preprocessing Pipeline
- Data cleaning (removing HTML tags, numerical values, symbols)
- Lowercasing
- Stop word removal
- Tokenization
- Lemmatization
- Deduplication (exact and near-duplicate detection; near-duplicates via pairwise cosine similarity on TF-IDF vectors, threshold ≥ 0.95)
- Overlap resolution (conflicting-label headlines flagged and manually inspected; unresolved ambiguity excluded)
- Noise filtering (titles with excessive typos, non-linguistic characters, or fewer than 3 words removed; out-of-domain samples excluded)
Feature extraction: TF-IDF with max_features = 10,000 and min_document_frequency = 10.
Data split: 80% training / 20% testing.

## Training Strategy
- ML models fine-tuned using GridSearchCV hyperparameter optimization.
- NLP model hyperparameters (transformer block/attention layer size, dropout, learning rates, optimizers) optimized using the Optuna framework.
- Ensemble transformer: CI-based weighting strategy using Wilson score interval; weights inversely proportional to CI width (w1 = 0.5010 for RoBERTa, w2 = 0.4990 for DeBERTa).
- Optimization applied: model pruning, dynamic quantization (signed 8-bit integer, via PyTorch + Hugging Face Transformers), and feature- + logit-based knowledge distillation.
- Knowledge distillation: DistilBERT-base student, RoBERTa-base teacher; AdamW optimizer, learning rate 3e-5, weight decay 0.01, warmup ratio 0.1, distillation temperature 2.0, equal loss weights (α = 0.5, β = 0.5) for feature and logit components.
- All models trained on Google Colab Pro+ (ensemble reported on an NVIDIA A100 GPU). Website retraining on local hardware (i5-6600K CPU, GTX 1050 GPU).

NLP models (Table 3): batch size = 16 for all; embedding layer sizes — BERT-base 30,522×768 (110M params, 417.70 MB); RoBERTa-base 50,265×768 (125M, 486.98 MB); DeBERTa-base 50,000×768 (140M, 543.81 MB); DistilBERT-base 30,522×768 (66M, 256 MB); XLNet-base-cased 32,000×768 (110M, 440 MB); Ensemble (RoBERTa+DeBERTa) 50,265×768 (265M, 1030 MB).

NLP models (Table 5), Accuracy / Precision / Recall / F1 (%):
- BERT: 95.84 / 95.85 / 95.84 / 95.84
- RoBERTa: 96.45 / 96.45 / 96.45 / 96.45
- DeBERTa: 96.42 / 96.41 / 96.42 / 96.42
- DistilBERT: 95.62 / 95.62 / 95.62 / 95.62
- XLNet: 95.93 / 95.97 / 95.93 / 95.95
- Ensemble (RoBERTa+DeBERTa) (best overall): 96.65 / 96.68 / 96.65 / 96.66

Optimization (Table 7): Ensemble without optimization: 1030.79 MB, 265M params, 23,400 s, 96.65% accuracy; with model pruning: 820 MB, 210M, 17,550 s, 92.78%; with quantization: 490.47 MB, 128M, 11,250 s, 95.68%; RoBERTa (teacher): 96.45%; DistilBERT-Student (feature+logits distillation): 256 MB, 66M, 5,830 s, 96.17% accuracy.
Pruning reduced parameters from 265M to 210M and improved training time by ~25%; quantization reduced size by >50% while maintaining 95.68% accuracy.
Ensemble confusion matrix: 100% AI–generated correctly classified, 91.00% fake, 93.00% real. Random Forest confusion matrix: 97.74% AI–generated, 92.35% real, 79.24% fake.
Website ("Integrity-Guard"): handled 9,326 POST requests, 0 failures, average response 113.6 ms, ~32 RPS.

## Strengths
- First study to jointly address fake, real, AND AI–generated news detection with a purpose-built three-class dataset (155,121 titles) combining five open-source datasets.
- Broad model comparison (6 ML + 5 NLP + ensemble transformer + distilled student).
- Reports detailed model efficiency work: pruning, dynamic quantization, and knowledge distillation with concrete size/parameter/time trade-offs.
- Provides interpretability via attention maps and per-class confusion matrices, plus a deployed Flask website with human-in-the-loop feedback retraining.
- Dataset and implementation scripts publicly released (GitHub: https://github.com/ishraqisheree99/Combined-News-Dataset.git).

## Weaknesses
- Classification operates only on news titles/headlines (not full article bodies), which may limit real-world robustness.
- The "AI–generated" class is largely from a single source (TurningBench) and self-generated LLM titles, which may make the class easy to separate (reported near-100% accuracy), potentially inflating overall metrics.
- Deduplication/overlap resolution involved some manual inspection, which is not fully reproducible.
- Optimized (pruned) ensemble accuracy drops notably (96.65% → 92.78%), a trade-off only partly addressed.

## Limitations
Stated by the authors (Section 4.1):
- Language limitation: research primarily focuses on English-language content; may not generalize to other languages.
- Evolving AI technologies: models need frequent updates to remain effective against new generation techniques.
- Real-time detection: study uses precollected datasets; ability to detect real-time/dynamic online content is limited.

## Future Work
Stated by the authors: create a multimodal diverse dataset using text, audio, and images across multiple languages; use image-captioning models (e.g., CLIP, ViLBERT) to detect text-image inconsistencies; use deepfake detection models (e.g., XceptionNet, FaceForensics++) for synthetic videos; employ a multimodal transformer model (e.g., MMFT-BERT) to fuse textual and visual features; extend to a health-misinformation framework with domain-specific datasets; incorporate article bodies, source credibility, and metadata; develop an efficient iOS/Android mobile application. Additionally, explore deep ensemble learning strategies such as stacking or adaptive weighting.

## Interesting Ideas
- CI-based (Wilson score interval) weighting for combining transformer predictions, with weights inversely proportional to CI width.
- Using an ensemble of open-source LLMs (GPT-2, GPT-NEO, Distil-GPT-2) with majority voting to synthesize additional AI–generated training data.
- Combining model pruning + dynamic quantization + feature/logit knowledge distillation to shrink an ensemble transformer for deployment.
- Human-in-the-loop feedback retraining scheduler triggered every 100 new samples.

## Possible Inspiration For Our Paper
- The three-class framing (real / fake / AI–generated) is directly relevant to a fake-news benchmark that wants to include machine-generated news as a category.
- The dataset-combination recipe (FakeNewsNet, ISOT, LIAR, COVID-19, TurningBench) plus rigorous dedup (cosine similarity ≥ 0.95 on TF-IDF) is a reusable benchmark-construction protocol.
- Efficiency benchmarking (params, size, training time vs. accuracy after pruning/quantization/distillation) is a useful axis for a benchmark beyond raw accuracy.

## Potential Citation Usage
- Cite as an example of a multi-source combined fake + AI–generated news dataset and three-class classification.
- Cite for ensemble transformer (RoBERTa+DeBERTa) results and for model compression trade-offs (pruning/quantization/distillation).
- Cite when motivating inclusion of AI–generated/LLM-synthesized text as a detection class in a benchmark.
